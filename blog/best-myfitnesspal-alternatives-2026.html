Quick answer

What you need to know

MacroSidekick is a strong MyFitnessPal alternative for iPhone users who want to log by natural speech and correct a whole meal conversationally. Cronometer is better for nutrient depth, MacroFactor for adaptive expenditure and targets, Lose It for a conventional approachable diary, FoodNoms for a focused Apple experience, and Fitia for meal planning. MyFitnessPal still makes sense for database reach and familiarity.

01

Do not switch because a list told you to

MyFitnessPal has two durable advantages: a broad food database and years of familiarity for many users. Saved meals, recipes, history, and learned habits have real value. A replacement needs to solve a recurring problem, not merely offer a newer home screen.

The useful reasons to leave tend to be specific. You may dislike searching for every ingredient in a mixed meal. You may want more detailed nutrient data, a coached macro target, meal suggestions, or a quieter Apple-focused product. Start there. This article is published by MacroSidekick, which is one of the products listed. We use official product sources and state where another app is a better fit.

02

Pick by priority

Membership details change, especially across regions and app stores. Treat price as a live checkout question. The recommendations here focus on product shape and daily workflow, which are more useful than a temporary discount.

03

1. MacroSidekick when search is the problem

MyFitnessPal works well when you know the food entry you need. It becomes more laborious when lunch is a bowl with six parts, a homemade sauce, and a portion you need to explain. MacroSidekick begins with the explanation. Speak naturally, type, or attach up to four photos, then review the proposed ingredients and nutrition before saving.

The correction loop is the important part. If the estimate shows one tablespoon of dressing and you used two, say so. If the bread was rye or half the fries stayed on the plate, correct the whole meal by voice or text. AI does not remove uncertainty, but an editable breakdown makes assumptions visible.

MacroSidekick's coach can use the user's plan, history, saved recipes, preferences, and remaining macros. It can log a meal or save a recipe from the conversation. The current product is a real iPhone beta distributed through TestFlight. Public launch pricing has not been announced, and there is no Android beta claim here.

04

2. MacroFactor when targets should adapt

MacroFactor is the strongest alternative on this list for someone who wants the app to estimate changing energy expenditure and update a coached macro program. According to its official product explanation, the calculation uses logged intake and changes in weight trend. Its weekly recommendations are designed to be adherence neutral.

That capability comes with an input requirement. MacroFactor notes that partially logged days can mislead the calculation because the app sees incomplete intake. If you can keep a sufficiently complete diary and care about adaptive targets, that trade makes sense. If you mainly want less logging work, evaluate the input flow before the algorithm.

05

3. Cronometer when nutrition detail is the point

Cronometer is the better fit for people who want to inspect more than calories, protein, carbohydrate, and fat. The Cronometer site puts comprehensive nutrient tracking and data quality at the center of the product. That emphasis is useful for someone who wants a more detailed view of dietary patterns.

More detail is not always more useful. If the extra nutrient panels will not change what you buy, cook, or discuss with a qualified professional, a simpler log may be easier to maintain. Cronometer wins this category because it specializes, not because every person needs the maximum number of fields.

06

4. Lose It when you still want a diary

Some people want MyFitnessPal without changing the mental model. Lose It remains a sensible option because its official site presents an approachable calorie budget and food log. The app is easier to evaluate when you ask, ‘Can I keep doing what I already do with less friction?’ rather than, ‘Does it have the most AI?’

Lose It is not a substitute for MacroFactor's expenditure model, and it is not centered on a conversational coach like MacroSidekick. That can be a benefit. A clear diary with familiar controls may be exactly what a user wants.

07

5. FoodNoms when the Apple ecosystem matters

FoodNoms is a focused food and nutrition tracker built around Apple platforms. Its public product materials emphasize speed, privacy, and Apple Health integration. That makes it worth considering if your phone, watch, and health data already live in Apple's ecosystem.

The narrower focus is also the tradeoff. It will not be the universal choice for households that share workflows across iOS and Android. Platform fit is mundane, but it matters every single day.

08

6. Fitia for plans, 7. Cal AI for a photo-first start

Fitia blends calorie and macro tracking with meal plans, food suggestions, and recipe-oriented tools. It is a stronger answer when the hard part happens before logging: deciding what to eat and fitting meals into targets. A person who wants guidance at the planning stage may get more value here than from another bigger diary.

Cal AI makes the meal photo a prominent starting point. That can feel lighter than database search, especially for quick estimates. Judge any photo-first app by what happens after capture. Can you see ingredients, change the portion, add hidden oil, and correct the result without fighting the interface? A photo is evidence, not a complete recipe.

09

When MyFitnessPal is still the better choice

Stay with MyFitnessPal if you use its broad database efficiently, rely on saved history, and are not bothered by the current membership split. The company documents Free, Premium, and Premium+ options on its membership page. Check that page and your regional checkout for the latest feature and price details.

Also stay if your proposed replacement does not support the foods, devices, or sharing patterns you actually use. Switching costs are rarely included in comparison charts. Rebuilding recipes and relearning serving controls can erase a small theoretical gain.

10

Run a four-meal trial

Before moving your routine, log four cases: a barcode item, a meal you cook from a recipe, a restaurant plate, and a repeated breakfast. Make one correction in each app. Look for serving clarity, visible assumptions, speed on the second attempt, and whether the summary shows the information you use.

Then make the narrow choice. MacroSidekick for conversational input, MacroFactor for adaptive targets, Cronometer for nutrient depth, Lose It for a familiar diary, FoodNoms for Apple focus, Fitia for planning, or Cal AI for a photo-led start. If MyFitnessPal still wins your test, keep it without apology.

A tracker only earns its place when its daily workflow is easier than the behavior it asks you to maintain.

Cronometer is the strongest fit in this group for detailed nutrient tracking. It makes comprehensive nutrition data a central product feature.
